# Product Requirements

This document consolidates the founder workshop. Ratings:
- 3 = absolutely essential
- 2 = important
- 1 = low importance
- 0 = not important

Nearly all evaluated requirements were rated 3. That means strategic importance, not immediate release order.

## 1. Why WorkCore exists
One coherent system for daily business work; reduce administration, time and avoidable mistakes; simple onboarding; cross-device; offline; useful AI; commercially scalable. **Priority 3**

## 2. Target groups
- Crafts/service: **3**
- Property/property service: **3**
- Consulting/office: **2**
- Agriculture: **2**
- Logistics: **1**
- Other suitable SMEs: **1**

Company sizes: solo, 2–5, 6–20, potentially 21–50.

## 3. Product values
Simplicity, clarity, reliability, trust, professional capability, design quality and useful AI. **3**

## 4. Recommendation / product pulse
Desired sentiment: “This moved us forward.” / “The employees love it.” **3**

## 5. Brand character
**Easy. Professional. Clear.** **3**

## 6. Product philosophy
Avoid overload and ambiguity; few nested menus; touch-friendly; few steps; fast onboarding; intelligent defaults; early error detection; next-step guidance; modular and multi-industry; fast, reliable and trustworthy. Mostly **3**.

## 7. Design, UX and AI principles
Consistent UI, useful empty states, autosave where safe, visible sync, handoff, offline, conflict handling, history/restore, export/import, privacy, roles, proactive but controlled AI. Mostly **3**. Some secondary design preferences rated **2**.

## 8. Architecture and security
All assessed requirements **3**: server source of truth, no silent overwrite, versions, unique IDs, soft delete, transactions, audit, least privilege, tenant isolation, encryption, MFA, secure sessions, tested backups, server-side rights, environment separation, export, resilience, monitoring, critical automated tests.

## 9. Modules and functionality
All assessed requirements **3**: customers, jobs, projects/objects, recurring jobs, planning, staff, time, materials/services, inventory, purchasing, suppliers, offers, invoices, payments/dunning, accounting integrations, documents, communication, service reports, signatures, media, vehicles, drive log, maintenance, tasks/checklists, calendar, email, portals, analytics, profitability, resources, contracts/deadlines, CRM, mobile field work, complaints, QA, knowledge base, tools/assets, barcode/QR, maps/routing, deliveries, expenses, absences, qualifications, subcontractors, approvals, custom fields/forms, industry profiles, multi-company, localization, white-label and API.

## 10. Pricing and marketing
Mostly **3**. Partner/reseller model **2**; money-back/goodwill **2**.

## 11. Support, onboarding and customer experience
Mostly **3**. Public knowledge base **2**; community/forum **2**; opt-in beta **2**.

## 12. Development, releases and quality
All **3**. See Quality-and-Release-Principles.md.

## 13. Legal, compliance and trust
All **3**. See Security-and-Compliance-Principles.md.

## 14. Strategy, brand and growth
All **3**: one platform/multiple industries, flexible WorkCore/Koll branding, SME focus, proper localization, simplicity + professional depth, trust, transparent capability limits, scalable brand system.

## 15. Technical platform and integrations
All **3**: API-first, webhooks, event architecture, backend business logic, module boundaries, shared core, unified models, API versioning, central auth, object storage, durable offline queue, idempotent actions, mutation IDs, optimistic UI + reconciliation, background/delta sync, conflict strategies, selective realtime, explicit sync states, resumable uploads, checksums, adapters, isolation of integration failures, job queue.

## 16. Data, search and reporting
All **3**: global/full-text search, suggestions, saved views, reporting, custom reports, permission-aware reporting, exports, schedules, period comparisons, drill-down, freshness caveats, targets, transparent formulas, custom KPIs, anomaly detection, forecasts, data quality indicators, central metric definitions.

## 17. Automation and workflows
All **3**: no-code automation, multi-step, conditions, multiple actions, scheduled/event-driven, human approvals, errors, simulation, versions, templates, permissions, preview, pause, manual run, history, dependency detection, loop/rate/mass-action protection, clear status.

## 18. AI in operational work
All **3**. See AI-Principles.md.

## 19. Mobile and field service
All assessed requirements **3**: full-value mobile, today/now home, one-hand use, camera, voice, offline, location only with benefit, quick actions, minimum typing, large targets, shortened forms, quick capture, slow-network resilience, calm offline indicator, mobile shortcuts, relevant notifications, deep links, continuity after app close/sleep/network changes.

## 20. Collaboration and teamwork
All assessed requirements **3**: assignments, comments, mentions, conflict-safe shared editing, handover, team overview, delegation, activity history, internal/external communication separation, shared files, team permissions, approvals, delegated approvals, resource-aware planning, contextual communication, personal work views, escalation, shift/day handover, team KPIs without surveillance, knowledge retention, less reliance on extra chats/spreadsheets/meetings.

## 21. Administration, configuration and company management
All assessed requirements **3**: central company settings, role-based admin, standards plus exceptions, audit, multi-company, user admin, secure offboarding, module activation, safe defaults, configuration transfer, test critical settings, detect invalid config, impact preview, rollback/versioning, templates and versions, standard processes, company required fields, admin health dashboard, simple administration despite flexibility.

## 22. Data migration, import and rollout
All assessed requirements **3**: import from existing systems, preview, intelligent field mapping, duplicate detection, rollback, staged migration, visible status, reusable mappings, import logs, post-import validation, dry runs, relationship preservation, staged batches, document/media migration, provenance, migration approval, controlled old-system cutover, rollout checklist and validated go-live.

## Conclusion
The product vision is intentionally broad. The next step is roadmap classification, not simultaneous implementation of all requirements.
